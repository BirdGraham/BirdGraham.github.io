The jump from arithmetic to letters is bigger than it looks

A small gap from earlier math, such as fractions, negative numbers or order of operations, can turn into a wall. Most students who say they are bad at algebra have one or two missing pieces, not a missing talent.

A Sample Problem

Why checking your answer matters

Algebra I · Two-step equation

Solve for \(x\): \(\dfrac{x}{3} - 5 = 4\)

Undo the subtraction first.
Add 5 to both sides: \(\dfrac{x}{3} = 9\).
Then undo the division.
Multiply both sides by 3: \(x = 27\).
Check it.
\(\dfrac{27}{3} - 5 = 9 - 5 = 4\) ✓ A ten-second check catches most sign and arithmetic slips before the teacher does.

The habit of checking every answer is one of the quickest ways to raise a math grade, and it carries straight into SAT and ACT math. Interested in test prep? See SAT & ACT math tutoring.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Algebra I and Algebra II help?

Algebra I covers the foundations: solving equations and inequalities, graphing lines, systems of equations, exponents and an introduction to quadratics. Algebra II builds on that with more advanced functions, including quadratics, polynomials, exponentials and rational expressions. Sessions follow whichever course your student is taking, and go back to earlier material whenever a gap is holding them up.

My child understands it in class but still struggles on tests. Why?

This is common. Following a teacher's example is different from starting a problem cold under time pressure. Sessions practice that second skill: choosing a method, setting the problem up, checking the answer, and recognizing the common mistakes, such as sign errors, that quietly cost points.

Can you help my student catch up or get ahead before the next course?

Yes. Catching up on missed concepts and previewing the next course are both common reasons families reach out. Sessions begin with an assessment of current skills, so time goes to what your student actually needs rather than a fixed curriculum.

My student is far behind grade level. Is that a problem?

Not at all. Algebra problems usually trace back to earlier skills such as fractions, negative numbers or order of operations. Sessions find those root gaps and fix them first, then move forward, which gives algebra something solid to stand on.

Do you tutor Geometry too?

Yes. Geometry tutoring is part of the same program, along with middle school math, SAT and ACT prep, ISEE prep and Regents prep.
