Step 3

How to use a practice test so your score actually goes up

Taking test after test without reviewing is the most common way students waste prep time. A better routine looks like this:

  1. Test like it is realSit down somewhere quiet, keep to the time limits and take the breaks. Practice under test conditions is what builds stamina and pacing.
  2. Score it with the scoring guideCount your correct answers and use the scoring guide to find your score range for each section.
  3. Sort every missFor each wrong answer, decide what happened: a concept you do not know, a setup mistake, a careless slip, or running out of time. Each type needs a different fix.
  4. Read the answer explanationsRead the explanation for every miss, and for any question you guessed on and got right. Then try to explain it back in your own words.
  5. Redo and practice the weak spotsWithin a couple of days, redo the missed questions without looking, then practice that skill on fresh problems before taking the next full test.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Where are the answer keys for the digital SAT practice tests?

College Board publishes an answer explanations PDF for each printable practice test. It gives the correct answer for every question and explains why the other choices are wrong. Each test also has a scoring guide that shows how to turn your number of correct answers into a score. All of them are linked in the table on this page and open on College Board's own site.

Are these practice tests free?

Yes. College Board offers its official practice tests, scoring guides and answer explanations at no charge, and the PDFs on this page can be opened and downloaded without an account. This page only links to them. The tests themselves belong to College Board and are not hosted here.

Should my student use Bluebook or the printable PDFs?

For most students, Bluebook is the better choice because it matches the real digital test, including the adaptive modules and the built-in Desmos calculator. College Board describes the printable versions as aimed mainly at students who will test on paper with accommodations. They are still useful for extra practice, but they are not adaptive, so scores may not line up perfectly with the real test.

How often should a student take a full practice test?

A common approach is one full test every one to two weeks while preparing, with the days in between spent reviewing mistakes and practicing the weak areas. Reviewing a test carefully is worth more than taking another one right away.
