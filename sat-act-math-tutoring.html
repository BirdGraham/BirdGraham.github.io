What the Math Sections Test

Mostly algebra, and it shows up everywhere

On the digital SAT, math questions fall into four content areas: Algebra, Advanced Math, Problem-Solving and Data Analysis, and Geometry and Trigonometry. The ACT draws on the same core ideas, including algebra, functions, geometry, trigonometry, and statistics and probability. In both tests, algebra fluency decides how much time is left for everything else, which is why prep here begins with finding exactly which skills are costing points.

The SAT's built-in graphing calculator is a real advantage for students who know when to use it. Some problems fall in seconds when you graph them, while others are faster by hand. Learning to tell the difference is part of the work.

Sample Problems

Two problems, two ways to think

SAT-style · Use the calculator wisely

How many points do the graphs of \(y = x^2 - 4x + 3\) and \(y = x - 1\) have in common?

By hand.
Set the expressions equal: \(x^2 - 4x + 3 = x - 1\), so \(x^2 - 5x + 4 = 0\). Factor: \((x - 1)(x - 4) = 0\), so \(x = 1\) or \(x = 4\). Two solutions means two points.
With Desmos.
Type both equations and count the intersections: two, at \((1, 0)\) and \((4, 3)\). That takes about ten seconds, and it is a good way to check the algebra.
Answer: 2. Knowing both routes means a student can pick the faster one, or use one to verify the other.
ACT-style · Look for the shortcut

What is the sum of the solutions to \(2x^2 - 14x + 12 = 0\)?

Factor it.
Pull out the 2: \(2(x^2 - 7x + 6) = 0\), then \(2(x - 1)(x - 6) = 0\). The solutions are 1 and 6, and \(1 + 6 = 7\).
The shortcut.
For any quadratic \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\), the solutions add up to \(-\dfrac{b}{a}\). Here that is \(\dfrac{14}{2} = 7\), with no factoring needed.
Answer: 7.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Should my student take the SAT or the ACT?

Colleges that ask for test scores generally accept either one, so the better choice is the test your student scores higher on and feels more comfortable taking. The quickest way to find out is to take a timed practice test of each and compare. The formats differ: the digital SAT adapts to a student's performance and has a built-in graphing calculator, while the ACT is not adaptive, offers four answer choices on every math question, and makes the Science section optional. A free consultation is a good place to talk through which one fits your student.

How is the math section of the digital SAT structured?

The SAT Math section has two modules of 22 questions each, with 35 minutes per module, so 70 minutes in total. How your student does on the first module determines whether the second module is easier or harder, and a module cannot be revisited once it is finished. About three-quarters of the questions are multiple choice and the rest are student-produced responses. A Desmos-based graphing calculator and a reference sheet are built into the testing app.

What changed on the ACT?

On the enhanced ACT, the Science section is optional, the math questions have four answer choices instead of five, and the Composite score is based on English, math and reading only. Students who take Science receive a separate Science and STEM score. The scale is still 1 to 36. Test formats can change, so it is worth checking act.org for the current details before you register.

When should we start, and how many sessions will we need?

It depends on where your student is starting, the target score, and the test date. A good rule of thumb is to begin two to three months before the test date, which leaves room for a diagnostic, targeted practice, and at least a couple of full practice tests. If your student's algebra foundations are shaky, starting earlier helps, because those gaps cost points on every section of math.

Do you guarantee a score improvement?

No. No honest tutor can promise a specific score. What you get is a clear picture of where your student is losing points, a plan aimed at those gaps, regular timed practice, and accountability, which are the things that move scores for students who put in the work.
